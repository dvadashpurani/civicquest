# CivicQuest mission roadmap

One mission per school day, Monday to Friday. Each week is one module of five missions. Every mission starts with a short story (Pip the fox for grades 2–5) or a case study (grades 6–12), then a discussion question, then the activity.

Use this file as the seed content for `modules` and `missions`. Store `story` and `ask` as new text columns on `missions`, and set `day_number` to 1–5.

Points: 10 per mission for Learners and Helpers, 15 for Leaders.

Verification mapping: "Teacher watched the class activity" = class_activity, "Short quiz, checked by the app" = quiz, "Photo of the place…" = photo_review, "QR check-in…" = qr_checkin, "The other class’s teacher confirms" = class_activity confirmed by the other teacher.

## Learners, grades 2–5

### Week 1: Safe Streets (stamp: Safety)

**Day 1 (Monday): Stop, look, listen**
- Story: Pip’s ball rolls into the street, and he runs after it without stopping. A car honks and stops just in time. Pip is safe, but very scared.
- Discussion question: What should Pip have done first?
- Students do: Learn the three steps for crossing a street: stop at the curb, look left, right, and left again, and listen for cars. Practice them together as a class.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: crosswalk

**Day 2 (Tuesday): Know your signals**
- Story: Pip sees the walk signal start blinking and runs anyway. The light changes when he’s only halfway across.
- Discussion question: What does a blinking hand mean?
- Students do: Learn what the walk sign, blinking hand, and steady hand mean, then play the signal game.
- Proof: Short quiz, checked by the app
- Town building: crosswalk

**Day 3 (Wednesday): Be seen**
- Story: Pip walks home at dusk in a dark jacket. A driver doesn’t see him until the very last second.
- Discussion question: How could Pip help drivers see him?
- Students do: Learn why bright colors and reflectors help drivers see you. Find something reflective in the classroom.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: bikelane

**Day 4 (Thursday): Bus safety**
- Story: Pip drops his homework near the school bus wheel and reaches under the bus to grab it.
- Discussion question: Who should Pip tell instead?
- Students do: Learn the danger zone around a bus: take ten giant steps away, never reach near the wheels, and tell the driver if you drop something.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: busstop

**Day 5 (Friday): My safe route**
- Story: Pip’s friend takes a shortcut through a parking lot where cars back out without seeing kids.
- Discussion question: Is the shortcut worth it?
- Students do: Draw a safe route from school to a nearby place, and mark every crosswalk on the way.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: bikelane

## Helpers, grades 6–8

### Week 1: Neighborhood Helpers (stamp: Helping)

**Day 1 (Monday): Spot the problem**
- Story: A streetlight near school has been out for weeks. Students walk past it in the dark after practice, but nobody reports it, because everyone assumes someone else already did.
- Discussion question: Why did nobody report it?
- Students do: As a class, list problems you’ve noticed near school, like broken lights, faded crosswalks, or cracked sidewalks.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: clinic

**Day 2 (Tuesday): Who fixes what**
- Story: A family reports a pothole to the school office, which can’t fix streets. The pothole stays for months.
- Discussion question: Who should they have told?
- Students do: Learn which problems go to the city’s 311 service, which go to the school, and which go to a property owner.
- Proof: Short quiz, checked by the app
- Town building: library

**Day 3 (Wednesday): Report it right**
- Story: A report that says “broken thing on my street” gets ignored. One with the exact location, a photo, and a clear description gets fixed.
- Discussion question: What makes a report useful?
- Students do: With your teacher, write a clear report for one problem near school, with a photo of the place.
- Proof: Photo of the place or the work, no faces, teacher approves
- Town building: busstop

**Day 4 (Thursday): Help a neighbor**
- Story: An older neighbor can’t carry groceries up the stairs since winter started, and she doesn’t want to ask anyone.
- Discussion question: How could a class help safely?
- Students do: Plan a safe way the class can help older neighbors, like a card drive or a food drive with a partner organization.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: foodbank

**Day 5 (Friday): Follow up**
- Story: Two weeks after a class reported a broken streetlight, it’s fixed, and students walk home safer.
- Discussion question: What changed because they spoke up?
- Students do: Check on the problem your class reported, and share what happened.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: bikelane

## Leaders, grades 9–12

### Week 1: Civics Project (stamp: Civic action)

Supports the Massachusetts student-led civics project requirement.

**Day 1 (Monday): Pick a problem**
- Story: Students at one high school notice there’s no crosswalk outside their school, even though hundreds of students cross the road every morning.
- Discussion question: What problem in our community matters to us?
- Students do: In groups, pick one non-partisan problem in your community that you want to work on.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: library

**Day 2 (Tuesday): Research it**
- Story: The group finds out who uses the road, how often near-misses happen, and which office is in charge of crosswalks.
- Discussion question: What do we need to know?
- Students do: Research the problem: who it affects, why it happens, and what has already been tried.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: library

**Day 3 (Wednesday): Make a plan and find who decides**
- Story: The group learns that crosswalks are decided by the city’s transportation department, with input from the city council.
- Discussion question: Who can actually make this change?
- Students do: Write an action plan, and find the person or office that can make the change, such as a city councilor or the school committee.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: school

**Day 4 (Thursday): Take action**
- Story: The group writes a short, polite letter with their research and presents it at a public meeting.
- Discussion question: What action fits our plan?
- Students do: Carry out your plan, such as a letter to a city official or a presentation to the school committee.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: school

**Day 5 (Friday): Share the results**
- Story: Months later, a new crosswalk is painted, and the group presents what they learned to younger students.
- Discussion question: What did we learn, and what’s next?
- Students do: Present your project and its results to the class.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: library

### Week 3: Know Your City (stamp: Civic action)

**Day 1 (Monday): Who runs your city**
- Story: Many people know the president’s name but not their city councilor’s, even though the council decides on parks, buses, and school budgets.
- Discussion question: Who represents you locally?
- Students do: Find your city councilor and school committee members, and what each one decides.
- Proof: Short quiz, checked by the app
- Town building: library

**Day 2 (Tuesday): How a decision gets made**
- Story: A town wants to add a bike lane. The idea goes through a proposal, a public meeting, public comments, and a vote.
- Discussion question: Where can residents have a say?
- Students do: Map the steps of one local decision, from idea to vote.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: bikelane

**Day 3 (Wednesday): Watch a public meeting**
- Story: Public meetings usually set aside time for residents to speak, but few young people ever use it.
- Discussion question: What got decided?
- Students do: Attend or watch a recorded city council or school committee meeting, and note one decision.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: school

**Day 4 (Thursday): Write to an official**
- Story: A short, clear, polite message with one specific request is more likely to get a reply than a long, angry one.
- Discussion question: What’s your one clear request?
- Students do: Draft a non-partisan letter to a local official about a community issue.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: school

**Day 5 (Friday): Read local news**
- Story: A local paper reports that the town library may cut its weekend hours.
- Discussion question: Who would this affect?
- Students do: Find one local news story and explain who it affects and who decides.
- Proof: Teacher watched the class activity
- Town building: firestation
